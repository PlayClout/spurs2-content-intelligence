03 / Rights before ranking

Cleaning is a loop, not a badge.

A model identifies visible and audible content. A versioned, rights-owner-approved policy determines permitted use for the chosen platform, territories, use, language, and release time.

Evaluatesource range + destination + policy
→
Remediate, if permittedmute / exclude shot / verified redaction
→
Verify + reevaluatenew derivative, new decision

What cleaning can do

  • Audio: remove restricted audio and inspect the actual output tracks.
  • Shots: exclude an unusable range while preserving the original source lineage.
  • Visual redaction: offer blur/crop only when policy allows it and every affected frame is verified. Advanced automation is deferred.
  • Audit: show the original issue, exact rule, transformation, verification, and final allowed context.

What it cannot clear

  • Embargo: hold or schedule; a crop cannot shorten a rights window.
  • Territory / platform / use: select an allowed destination or exclude the clip.
  • Unknown policy: route to authorized review, rather than defaulting to green.
  • New variant: dubbing, subtitles, audio changes, and trim expansions need a new evaluation.
Current-code gap: territory restrictions are currently surfaced without evaluating a requested territory, and approval blocks only the blocked state. The implementation must require a current destination-specific clearance, not merely a badge that is not red.

The sample hook is eligible for this prototype. Real captions need an authoritative source and editor review.
What a “20-year record” requires

Define whose record, the metric, opponent, competition, exact date window, comparison population, ties, and completeness. Without complete comparison coverage, suppress the superlative and offer a neutral headline. A changed scorer/result invalidates dependent captions and exports.

Stats are computed from sourced structured data. An LLM can suggest a query or phrase a verified result; it cannot supply missing history.
